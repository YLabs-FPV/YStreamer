## Hardware

- **Raspberry Pi 4 Model B.** The only compatible board so far.
- **A microSD card**, 16 GB or more. With the ready-made image, the system
  takes 8 GB and the rest of the card holds the settings and recordings, on a
  partition of their own. A card under about 10 GB works, but keeps everything
  on one partition.
- **A way to power the Pi** that doesn't use its USB-C port (see below).
- **A video source**: supported DJI goggles with a **USB-C to USB-C data
  cable**, or a USB camera.
- Optional: an HDMI screen, a USB drive for recordings, a
  [record button](/docs/using/record-button).

## Powering the Pi

The Pi 4 is normally powered through its USB-C port, but YStreamer needs that
port for the goggles. Power it another way:

- **The GPIO header**: a 5 V supply wired to a 5 V pin (pin 2 or 4) and a
  ground pin (for example pin 6). Use a supply rated for at least 3 A.
- **A PoE HAT**, powering the Pi over Ethernet.
- **A USB-C power and data splitter**, which feeds power into the USB-C port
  while passing data through to the goggles. This works, but isn't
  recommended.

  A supply that can't keep up causes glitches and USB drop-outs, including the

  dipped.

## Cables

- **Goggles**: a USB-C to USB-C cable that carries data.
- **DJI FPV Goggles V1 / V2** and **USB cameras** go in one of the Pi's
  USB-A ports instead.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Early Raspberry Pi 4 boards">
  The first Raspberry Pi 4 boards, **revision 1.1** from 2019, have a [USB-C
  wiring defect](https://hackaday.com/2019/07/16/exploring-the-raspberry-pi-4-usb-c-issue-in-depth/):
  both of the port's configuration pins share one resistor, against the USB-C
  standard. With those boards, the goggles may not connect over a USB-C to
  USB-C cable. **Revision 1.2 and later** are fixed. **System → About → Board**
  shows yours, for example "Raspberry Pi 4 Model B Rev 1.1".

  If yours is affected, use an **OTG adapter** (USB-C plug to USB-A socket)
  instead. Plug the adapter into the **goggles**, then run a USB-A to USB-C
  cable from it to the Pi's USB-C port. The adapter always goes on the goggles'
  side, never on the Pi's.
</Admonition>

## SD card and USB drives

The SD card holds the system, the settings and, unless you record elsewhere,
every recording, so it's written to constantly. Cheap cards wear out or slow
down under that.

- **A high-endurance microSD card**, the kind sold for dash cams and security
  cameras, lasts much longer under constant recording. Otherwise, pick one
  rated **A2** or at least **A1**, from a known brand.
- **16 GB or more.** With the ready-made image, everything past the first
  8 GB is for settings and recordings.
- **For long recordings, use a USB drive** in one of the **blue USB 3 ports**.
  It's usually faster than the SD card, and spares it the wear. Format it as **exFAT**
  to hold files over 4 GB; FAT32 works but splits recordings at about 3.5 GB.

## Supported inputs

| Device                                        | Port  | Status       |
| --------------------------------------------- | ----- | ------------ |
| DJI Goggles 2                                 | USB-C | Supported    |
| DJI Goggles Integra                           | USB-C | Supported    |
| DJI Goggles 3                                 | USB-C | Supported    |
| DJI Goggles N3                                | USB-C | Supported    |
| DJI FPV Goggles V1 / V2                       | USB-A | Experimental |
| USB cameras, capture sticks, analog receivers | USB-A | Supported    |

Which [camera settings](/docs/using/camera-settings) you can change depends on the
air unit.

For the setups that have actually been tested, see [tested hardware](/docs/getting-started/tested-hardware).

## Installation

- **The ready-made image [Recommended]**: Raspberry Pi OS Lite with
  YStreamer installed; flash, plug & play.
- **Install script**: for adding YStreamer to a Pi that already runs
  **Raspberry Pi OS Lite (64-bit, Debian 13 "Trixie")**.

## Limits

- **Raspberry Pi 4 Model B only.** More board support will be added
  in the future.
- **The USB-C port is reserved for the goggles** while YStreamer is
  installed.
- **Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only, on purpose.** 5 GHz is where the goggles' video
  link runs, and the Pi's Wi-Fi there would interfere with it.
- **No desktop.** Raspberry Pi OS with a desktop isn't supported: the desktop
  keeps the HDMI output for itself. Use the Lite version.
