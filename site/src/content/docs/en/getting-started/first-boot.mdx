<Admonition type="info" title="The first start takes longer">
  On the ready-made image, the first start sets up the SD card: it makes a
  partition for the settings and recordings from the rest of the card. On a
  large card that can take a few minutes. Later starts are quick.
</Admonition>

## 1. Connect to the device

There are two ways in. Either way, the web interface is at
**http://ystreamer.local**.

### Over Ethernet

Plug the Pi into your router with a cable. It gets an address from the router,
so any phone or computer on your network can open **http://ystreamer.local**.

### Over its own Wi-Fi

A fresh image also starts its own Wi-Fi network, so it works with nothing else
around, for example in the field:

- **Network:** `YStreamer`
- **Password:** `ystreamer`

Join it from a phone or laptop and open **http://ystreamer.local**.

### If ystreamer.local doesn't open

The `.local` name relies on your phone or computer looking it up on the
network, which some devices, Android phones in particular, don't do. Use the
address instead:

- On the device's own Wi-Fi, it's always **http://10.0.0.1**.
- Over Ethernet, it's the one your router gave it. The HDMI screen shows it,
  and so does your router's list of devices.

### With an HDMI screen

If a screen is plugged in, it shows everything above: the Wi-Fi network and
its password, a QR code to join it, and each address to open.

## 2. Set a password

The web interface has no password until you set one: anyone who can reach
the device can change its settings. Go to **System → General → Access** and
choose **Set password…**. See [password and SSH](/docs/network/security).

## 3. Pick your Wi-Fi country

Under **Network → Connectivity**, choose the country you're in. The Wi-Fi
radio follows the local rules for it.

## 4. Join your Wi-Fi (optional)

Still under **Network**, turn on **Join a network**, pick yours with **Scan**,
enter its password and **Save**. Only 2.4 GHz networks are listed.

Your browser drops off the YStreamer network when the Pi switches over.
Reconnect to your own Wi-Fi and open **http://ystreamer.local**. If the Pi
can't join within a minute, it brings its own network back so you can fix the
settings. See [Wi-Fi and Ethernet](/docs/network/wifi-and-ethernet).

## 5. Connect the goggles

YStreamer starts set up for **DJI Goggles 2, Goggles Integra, Goggles 3 and
Goggles N3**. Plug them into the Pi's **USB-C** port and turn the aircraft on.
The live view appears on the **Watch** page and on HDMI.

<Admonition type="info" title="Other goggles or a USB camera">
  For the first **DJI FPV Goggles (V1 / V2)**, or a **USB camera**, capture
  stick or analog receiver, switch the source under **Input → Video source**
  first, then plug it into one of the Pi's **USB-A** ports instead. See
  [video input](/docs/using/video-input).
</Admonition>
