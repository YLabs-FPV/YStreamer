A button or switch on the Pi's GPIO header can start and stop
[recording](/docs/using/recording), with an optional light that's on while it
records.

## Wiring

- **Button or switch**: between the chosen GPIO pin and any ground pin. No
  resistor needed: the Pi's own pull-up is used.
- **Light (optional)**: an LED from its GPIO pin, through a resistor of about
  330 Ω, to ground. The resistor can go on either side of the LED, but the LED
  has to be the right way round: its **longer leg (+)** toward the GPIO pin,
  the shorter one toward ground. The other way round, it just stays dark.

In this example the button is on GPIO17 (pin 11) with ground on pin 9, and the
light on GPIO18 (pin 12) with ground on pin 14.

## Settings

Under **Recording → Settings → Record button**:

| Setting                | What it does                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Use a button or switch | Turns it on.                                                                                 |
| Type                   | **Push button**: each press starts or stops. **Switch**: records for as long as it's closed. |
| Pin                    | The GPIO the button is on. GPIO17 (pin 11) by default.                                       |
| Recording light        | The GPIO for the light, or **No light**.                                                     |

Once it's on, the card says **Listening**. Press the button or flip the
switch to see it register.

With a **switch** that's already closed at power-on, for example after a
power cut, recording starts again by itself.

<Admonition type="warning">
  Do not use pin 40, it is the reset pin: holding it to ground while the Pi starts resets the
  settings.\
  See [backup and reset](/docs/maintenance/backup-and-reset).
</Admonition>
