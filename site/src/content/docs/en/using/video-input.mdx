Pick the source under **Input → Video source**. One is used at a time.

## DJI goggles

For **Goggles 2, Integra, Goggles 3 and N3**. Connect them to the Pi's
**USB-C** port with a USB-C data cable. You get the video, the air unit's
[camera settings](/docs/using/camera-settings), the goggles' battery and the link
quality.

The video is the goggles' own H.264 stream. YStreamer passes it on as it is,
without encoding it again.

<Admonition type="tip" title="No OSD in the feed">
  To keep the OSD out of the video, turn off **Camera View Recording** in the
  goggles, under Settings → Camera → Advanced Camera Settings.
</Admonition>

## FPV Goggles V1 / V2

The first DJI FPV Goggles. Connect them to one of the Pi's **USB-A** ports.
Video only: no camera settings, battery or link quality.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Experimental">
  This source hasn't been tried on real hardware by the maintainers yet.
</Admonition>

The card shows what it sees:

- **Receiving video.**
- **Goggles found, no video yet**: power the aircraft and wait for the
  picture in the goggles.
- **No goggles found**: check the cable and the port.

## USB camera

Webcams, HDMI capture sticks and analog receivers, in one of the **USB-A**
ports.

| Setting            | What it does                                                             |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Device             | Which camera. **First one found** uses whichever is plugged in.          |
| Resolution         | A mode the camera offers. **Best available** goes up to 1080p at 30 fps. |
| Bitrate (kbit/s)   | 500 to 20000. About 6000 for 1080p, 2500 for an analog receiver.         |
| Keyframe every (s) | 1 to 10. Shorter means players start sooner after joining.               |

**Refresh** looks for cameras again after you plug one in.

The camera's picture is encoded to H.264 by the Pi's hardware encoder. A
camera that sends H.264 itself is passed through as it is, and its own
bitrate applies.

<Admonition type="info" title="Software encoding">
  If the hardware encoder isn't available, the Input page says so and encoding
  falls back to the CPU, at 720p at most. That's usually too little video
  memory: add `gpu_mem=256` to `/boot/firmware/config.txt` and reboot. The image
  and the install script already set it.
</Admonition>

## RTMP stream

Another device streams to YStreamer over the network: OBS, a phone app such
as Larix Broadcaster, or DJI Fly's live streaming. Its video then goes to
HDMI, the browser, the streams and recordings, like the goggles' video does.

The app must be on the same network as the device: the same Wi-Fi, or joined
to YStreamer's access point.

1. Pick **RTMP stream** under **Input → Video source** and **Save**. A random
   stream key is made the first time.
2. The **RTMP stream** card shows the address to stream to, like
   `rtmp://192.168.1.50/live/<key>`. **Copy** copies it with the key.
3. Enter it in the app. Apps that ask for a server and a stream key separately
   take `rtmp://192.168.1.50/live` as the server and the key on its own.

The card says when an app is connected and when video arrives. The splash
screen shows until the first keyframe.

| Setting    | What it does                                                                                              |
| ---------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Stream key | At least 10 characters. Only an app that knows it can stream here. **New** makes another; save to use it. |
| Port       | 1935, what apps expect when the address has none. Or 1024 to 65535.                                       |
| Only from  | IP addresses allowed to stream, comma separated. Empty lets in any device with the key.                   |

One app streams at a time; a second one is turned away until the first stops.
Changing the key or **Only from** drops the app streaming now.

An app has 10 seconds from connecting to start streaming with the right key.
After 3 wrong keys from one address, that address is turned away for a
minute, and for twice as long after each further wrong key, up to 15 minutes.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Use the IP address">
  Many phone apps can't find `ystreamer.local`. Open the web interface by the
  device's IP address, and the card shows the address with it.
</Admonition>

### OBS

1. **Settings → Stream**: set **Service** to **Custom**, **Server** to
   `rtmp://192.168.1.50/live` and **Stream Key** to the key.
2. **Settings → Output**, with **Output Mode** on **Advanced**: pick an H.264
   encoder, set **Keyframe Interval** to 1 or 2 s, and for x264 the **Tune**
   to `zerolatency`, which leaves out B-frames.
3. **Start Streaming**.

### Phone apps and DJI Fly

Look for a custom RTMP server in the app's streaming settings and enter the
address from the card. In DJI Fly that's the live streaming option with
**RTMP**.

### What it takes

- **H.264**, up to 1080p. H.265, AV1 and the rest are turned away, and the card
  says which codec the app sent. Set the app to H.264.
- **No B-frames**, for the least delay. They still play, but decoders hold
  pictures back. The card warns when the stream has them.
- **Short keyframe intervals**, 1 to 2 s. Players and the splash screen wait
  for a keyframe.
- The app's **sound is left out**: no output carries it.

<Admonition type="info" title="Ports">
  The RTMP input listens on TCP port 1935, only while it's the selected source.
  It doesn't collide with the RTSP server (554), SRT (9000) or the RTMP
  _output_, which connects out to a server and opens no port.
</Admonition>
