A Raspberry Pi 4 can run several outputs at once, but not everything is free.
When the video stutters or lags, it's usually one of the things below. The
**Performance** page shows what's happening: processor load, temperature, frame
rate, bitrate and how many viewers and outputs are running.

## What costs what

With **DJI goggles**, the video arrives already compressed, and most outputs
only repackage it:

| Output                         | Cost     | Why                                               |
| ------------------------------ | -------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| RTSP, SRT, UDP, recording      | Low      | The video is passed on without being re-encoded   |
| RTMP                           | Low      | The same, plus a small silent audio track if it's on |
| HDMI                           | Moderate | The Pi's hardware decoder turns the video into a picture |
| Each browser watching          | Moderate | Every viewer gets their own copy of the stream    |

With a **USB camera**, YStreamer first has to compress its picture, which is
the heaviest job of all. The Pi's hardware encoder normally does it. Without
it, the CPU does it and is limited to 720p. See [video input](/docs/using/video-input).

## Keeping it smooth

- **Turn off what you don't use.** UDP, SRT and RTMP keep working while
  they're on, whether or not anyone receives them.
- **Turn off HDMI** under **Display → HDMI** when no screen is plugged in. It
  frees the decoder.
- **Cap the browser viewers** under **Streaming → Browser → Viewer limit**.
  For a crowd, stream to a server or platform and let it serve them instead.
- **Use a strong power supply.** An undersized one makes the Pi slow down and
  drops the USB link to the goggles. The Performance page warns when the
  voltage dipped. See [what you need](/docs/getting-started/requirements).
- **Keep it cool.** The Pi slows itself down when it gets too hot. A heatsink
  or a small fan helps, especially in a case or in the sun; the Performance
  page shows the temperature.
- **Prefer Ethernet for streams.** The Pi's Wi-Fi is 2.4 GHz only and shared
  with everything on it, including the access point's clients.
- **For a USB camera**, a lower resolution or bitrate under **Input** lightens
  the encoder's load.

<Admonition type="tip">
  Open the **Performance** page in one browser tab while you try something in
  another: the charts show straight away what each change costs.
</Admonition>
