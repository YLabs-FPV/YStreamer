With **DJI goggles** as the source, the **Camera** panel on the Watch page
changes the air unit's camera settings: the same ones as in the goggles'
menu, without putting the goggles on.

It's available once the aircraft is linked to the goggles. Until then it says
it's waiting for the aircraft.

## Settings

| Section          | Settings                                                 |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exposure         | Auto or manual, ISO, shutter, exposure compensation      |
| White balance    | Auto, or a colour temperature in kelvin                  |
| Video format     | Resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate                     |
| Image processing | Colour profile, anti-flicker, sharpness, noise reduction |

These are the air unit's camera settings, for the recording its own camera
makes on the aircraft. The live feed YStreamer receives comes over the radio
link, and its quality depends mostly on that link.

## Depends on the air unit

What's offered follows the air unit. For example:

- **O4 Air Unit Pro**: 1080p, 2.7K and 4K at every frame rate. 4:3 needs
  60 fps or less. Flat profile: D-Log.
- **O4 Air Unit**: 1080p, and 4K at 30, 50 and 60 fps.
- **O3 Air Unit**: 1080p, 2.7K and 4K. No 4:3 in 4K. Flat profile:
  D-Cinelike.

For an air unit it doesn't know, YStreamer offers everything and the air unit
refuses what it can't do.

<Admonition type="note">
  On manual exposure, the shutter speed can't be slower than the frame rate
  allows; the panel shows the slowest one.
</Admonition>
