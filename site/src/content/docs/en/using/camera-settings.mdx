With **DJI goggles** as the source, the **Camera** panel on the Watch page
changes the air unit's camera settings: the same ones as in the goggles'
menu, without putting the goggles on.

It's available once the aircraft is linked to the goggles. Until then it says
it's waiting for the aircraft.

## Settings

| Section          | Settings                                                 |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Exposure         | Auto or manual, ISO, shutter, exposure compensation      |
| White balance    | Auto, or a colour temperature in kelvin                  |
| Video format     | Resolution, aspect ratio, frame rate                     |
| Image processing | Colour profile, anti-flicker, sharpness, noise reduction |

These are the air unit's camera settings, for the recording its own camera
makes on the aircraft. The live feed YStreamer receives comes over the radio
link, and its quality depends mostly on that link.

## Depends on the air unit

What's offered follows the air unit. For example:

- **O4 Air Unit Pro**: 1080p, 2.7K and 4K at every frame rate. 4:3 needs
  60 fps or less. Flat profile: D-Log.
- **O4 Air Unit**: 1080p, and 4K at 30, 50 and 60 fps.
- **O3 Air Unit**: 1080p, 2.7K and 4K. No 4:3 in 4K. Flat profile:
  D-Cinelike.

For an air unit it doesn't know, YStreamer offers everything and the air unit
refuses what it can't do.

<Admonition type="note">
  On manual exposure, the shutter speed can't be slower than the frame rate
  allows; the panel shows the slowest one.
</Admonition>

## Gimbal

Turn on **Gimbal control** under **Input → Aircraft controls** and the Camera
panel gets a **Gimbal** section. It's off until you turn it on, because while
the browser holds the gimbal, the remote can't move it.

- **Avata 2**: press **Take control**, then set the tilt. **Hand back** gives
  the gimbal back to the remote. So do hiding or closing the page and losing
  the link, and the device hands it back by itself a second and a half after
  the browser stops sending.
- **Avata 360**: **View heading** turns the view around the sphere, like
  dragging the view in DJI Fly. The remote keeps its own tilt control.

The aircraft doesn't report the gimbal's angle back, so the panel shows the
angle you asked for.

### With a gamepad

Any gamepad the browser lists in the standard layout works. Press a button on
it once so the browser connects it, then:

| Control     | Avata 2                       | Avata 360                  |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Right stick | Tilt, up and down             | Turn the view left / right |
| Y / △       | Take control, or hand it back | -                          |
| A / ✕       | Level the camera              | Face forward               |
| B / ○       | Hand back                     | -                          |

<Admonition type="warning" title="Experimental">
  Gimbal control and the Avata 360 controls haven't been tried on a YStreamer Pi
  by the maintainers yet.
</Admonition>

## Avata 360

The goggles don't tell YStreamer when the aircraft is an Avata 360, so turn on
**Avata 360** under **Input → Aircraft controls**. The Camera panel then gets
an **Avata 360 lens** section:

- **Field of view**: from 104°, the flattest view, to 272°, the whole sphere.
  The same control as DJI Fly's zoom.
- **Vision Assist**: the aircraft's second camera, on or off, as in DJI Fly.
  YStreamer doesn't show that camera's picture.

Neither is reported back by the aircraft, so both show "—" until you set them.
