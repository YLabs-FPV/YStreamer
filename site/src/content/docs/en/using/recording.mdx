YStreamer records the video exactly as it arrives: the goggles' own stream,
or the USB camera's encoded picture.

<Admonition type="info" title="The live view, not the air unit's recording">
  With DJI goggles, YStreamer gets the live feed the goggles receive over the
  air, and records it as it is. That's the picture you see in the goggles, with
  its glitches and drop-outs, not the air unit's own recording, which is made on
  the aircraft at a much higher quality. For your best footage, record on the
  air unit too. YStreamer's recording is the live view, saved.
</Admonition>

## Start and stop

- The **Recorder** on the Watch page, or `Shift`+`R`.
- **Record automatically**: recording starts when video arrives and stops
  10 seconds after it goes, so each flight gets its own file. Stopping by hand
  sticks until the next video.
- A [record button](/docs/using/record-button) wired to the Pi.

## Settings

Under **Recording → Settings**. They apply to the next recording you start.

| Setting              | What it does                                                                                 |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Save to              | The SD card, a plugged-in USB drive, or **Somewhere else…** for any folder.                  |
| Format               | **MP4** plays anywhere. **MPEG-TS** is slightly bigger. Both survive a power cut mid-file.   |
| New file             | One long file, or a new file every 5, 10, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. Every 10 minutes by default. |
| File name prefix     | Letters, digits, `-` and `_`. `ystreamer` by default.                                        |
| Record automatically | See above.                                                                                   |
| Keep free            | Space to leave on the drive: 250 MB to 5 GB. 500 MB by default.                              |

Files are named `<prefix>-<date>-<time>.mp4`, with `-000`, `-001` and so on
added when they're split.

<Admonition type="info" title="The clock">
  The Pi has no clock of its own and sets the time from the internet. Without a
  connection, file names can carry the wrong date. They use the timezone under
  **System → General → Timezone**, UTC until you set it.
</Admonition>

## USB drives

Plug a drive into a USB-A port and it's mounted by itself, then offered under
**Save to** with its free space.

- On a FAT32 drive, files are split at about 3.5 GB, below FAT32's 4 GB limit.
- Before unplugging the drive, press **Eject** next to **Save to**, so
  everything is written.
- If the chosen drive isn't plugged in, recording doesn't start, rather than
  filling the SD card instead.

## Running out of space

Recording won't start, and stops, when the free space drops below **Keep
free**. The **Files** tab shows what the recordings take up and what's left.

## Files

**Recording → Files** lists the recordings, newest first, in grid or list
view. You can play them in the browser, download them or delete them.

With a USB drive plugged in, the menu at the top switches between the
recordings on the SD card and on the drive.

## Copying to a USB drive

To take recordings off the device without downloading them one by one, plug in
a USB drive and copy them across:

1. Open **Recording → Files**. The bar above the recordings has **To** and the
   drive's name.
2. Tick the recordings you want and press **Copy** or **Move**, or press
   **Copy everything new** for every recording the drive doesn't have yet.
3. The bar shows the progress. When it's done, press **Eject** before
   unplugging the drive.

It works the other way round too: switch the menu at the top to the drive, and
copy or move its recordings to the SD card.

- **Move** removes each original once its copy is complete. If a copy fails,
  nothing is removed.
- Recordings already on the drive are skipped. A different recording with the
  same name is copied as `name (2)`; nothing is overwritten.
- **Cancel** stops right away. Recordings already copied stay on the drive;
  the one halfway through is deleted, so a cut-short copy is never left behind
  under a recording's name. The same goes for a drive pulled out or a power
  cut mid-copy.
- Copying can run while the device records. Recording comes first, so the
  copy slows down meanwhile. The file being recorded is left out until it's
  finished.
- A FAT32 drive can't hold files over 4 GB. Use a drive formatted as exFAT for
  long recordings in one file.
- One transfer runs at a time, and recordings can't be deleted until it's done.
  It carries on if you close the page.

## Where the files are

Without the web interface, they're in `/var/lib/ystreamer/recordings`, or on
the USB drive.

On the ready-made image, settings and recordings live on a partition of their
own, so a power cut while recording can't damage the system. That partition is
formatted for Linux (ext4): Windows and macOS can't read it if you put the SD
card in a computer. Use a USB drive, or download the recordings, to get them
onto a computer.
